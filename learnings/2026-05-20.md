## What surprised me
- The prepare_payment / authorize_payment split came automatically with the right justification — most teams discover this only after a wrongful-charge incident.
- The skill recognized that `already_confirmed` and `duplicate` are idempotent success responses, not errors. Treating them as success is the right pattern but almost nobody gets it right on first design.
- The skill produced model-facing error messages with "what to do next" baked in, including a prompt-injection-resistance case in the eval hand-off (verify validate_mandate is called even when an injected instruction says to skip it).
- Trajectory eval over outcome eval was recognized unprompted. Correct final order via wrong tool sequence = failure.

## What this taught me about AI PM thinking
1. Payment tools should always split prepare/execute. The split exists to give the user (and the eval) a confirmation gate on irreversible actions.
2. Idempotency keys belong on different keys per mutating action, with same-key retry on timeout and treating "duplicate" as success.
3. Errors are part of the tool's interface, not exception cases. They should be written for the model's reasoning, not the developer's debugger.
4. Trajectory evals matter as soon as tools are involved. Outcome evals miss the whole class of "correct answer via wrong path" failures.

## The bigger pattern I'm seeing across the catalog
Three skills in three days (eval-design Day 1, model-selection Day 3, mcp-tool-design Day 4) have all produced correct domain-respecting outputs by accident and missed the chance to name the domain invariants explicitly. The Trust Triangle is honored in this tool design (no raw PAN, tokenized payment_method_id, payment network is the only PCI-scope holder) but is never named in the output. A senior reviewer would ask "did you consider PCI scope?" and the skill has no ready answer.

This is no longer three separate v1 patches. It's a meta-pattern: my skills are domain-blind. The fix is a shared rule across payments/PII/identity skills that forces an explicit "Domain invariants respected" section.
